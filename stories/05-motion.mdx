# Motion

Mechanical, not springy. A state change in this system should read as a relay closing, not as
something gliding into place. Spring physics and long eases belong to a different anchor.

## Durations

Short by default. `--default-transition-duration` is 110ms, so a bare `transition-colors` is already
fast without anything being specified.

## Easing

`--ease-snap` is `steps(3, end)`. It advances through three discrete frames instead of interpolating,
which is the anchor's characteristic movement — visibly stepped, like a mechanical counter. It is the
right choice for discrete state (a toggle flipping, a segment activating) and the wrong choice for
anything continuous such as a scroll-linked effect or a progress bar.

`--ease-mech-out` is the workhorse for entrances and is the project's
`--default-transition-timing-function`. `--ease-mech-in` is its counterpart for exits.

## Reduced motion

The base layer honours `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` globally: animation and transition durations
collapse to `0.01ms`, iteration counts drop to 1, and smooth scrolling is switched off. Every
component inherits this, so none of them need their own guard.

Two things worth knowing:

- `0.01ms` rather than `0` because some transitions rely on a `transitionend` event firing, and a
  zero-duration transition may not emit one.
- Next 16 no longer overrides a global `scroll-behavior: smooth` during navigation. Opting in
  requires `data-scroll-behavior="smooth"` on `<html>`, which `app/layout.tsx` sets and
  `app/globals.css` scopes the rule to — behind the same reduced-motion guard.

## Existing keyframes

`shadcn/tailwind.css` already ships `accordion-down`, `accordion-up`, the `scroll-fade-*` family and
`tw-shimmer`, plus `no-scrollbar`, `scroll-fade` and `shimmer` utilities. Build on those rather than
defining competing keyframes — the `shimmer` utility already carries its own reduced-motion guard.
