# News

## Problem

There is nowhere on this site to publish writing.

Spec 0002 built a single-scroll home page and listed "a blog, writing, or an RSS feed" as out of
scope, alongside "any route other than `/`". Both were the right call at the time: the content that
existed was three CVs, and a blog with nothing in it is worse than no blog.

That has changed. There is writing to publish, and the site has no shape for it. The page as built
is a record — what was made, where, and when. It says nothing about how any of it is thought about,
which is the part a reader deciding whether to work with someone actually wants.

The site also has a second, quieter problem that this work has to fix in passing: it was built as
one page, and it shows. The header, the focusable main region and the footer are composed inside the
home page rather than around every route, and every navigation link is a bare fragment. Adding any
second page exposes both — a route with no chrome, a skip link pointing at nothing, and a header
whose links go nowhere.

## Solution

Two new pages and one new band.

**`/news`** lists every **dispatch** newest-first: its date, its headline, its standfirst, its
topics, and its cover where it has one. Each row is entirely clickable and leads to the piece.

**`/news/<slug>`** is one dispatch — a way back, the date, the headline, the standfirst, the topics,
the cover, then the body.

**Band 07 on the home page** shows the three most recent and links to the rest. Contact moves from
07 to 08 and stays last.

A dispatch's body is authored as MDX. Its metadata — slug, date, headline, standfirst, topics,
cover — is typed data in the content layer, alongside every other fact on the site.

### The index is a log, not a gallery

A reverse-chronological dated list is a specific thing, and three plausible treatments were
available. Two were rejected on what they would say rather than on how they would look.

A **card grid** is for a set of peers where position carries no meaning — which is exactly what the
Projects band is, four platforms that ran concurrently. Reverse chronology is the opposite: a two-up
grid reads a dated list left-right-left-right, which is out of order, and orphans the odd item.
Cards also stop working somewhere past a handful, and an index grows without bound.

A **timeline** already means something here. Its rail and its markers are spent on the three roles,
with the signal marking the one that is current. Reusing that vocabulary for dispatches would imply
the same kind of record — spans, currency — and there is no current dispatch to mark.

So the index is **hairline-ruled rows**: the third structural device the system already has, after
the corner-ticked card and the hairline-gutter grid. A datasheet's log is ruled rows, and the date
sits in a fixed left column so the dates line up down the page edge and the eye tracks them — the
rail idea, applied inside a list.

### The whole row is the target

The engagement card has carried a promise since it was written: when detail pages exist, the whole
card becomes the target, not a "read more" bolted onto the corner. This is where that promise comes
due.

The row is clickable in full, and the link is still named by the headline alone. The date, the
standfirst, the topics and the cover are row content rather than link text, so the link announces as
"One direction, held" and not as "24 Aug 2026 One direction, held, notes on…". Because the row is
the hit area, the row is also what shows the focus ring.

### Covers are optional, and the alignment is not

A dispatch may carry a cover. It appears full-width on the piece itself, where it is the largest
element, and as a thumbnail in the index.

The index reserves the thumbnail column whether or not a row has one. A row that reclaimed the empty
space would make a mixed list read as broken rather than as optional; keeping it reserved means
every headline in the list starts and ends at the same place. On a phone the thumbnail is dropped
entirely — a 16:9 image stacked into a phone-width row triples its height for decoration, and this
system already keeps decoration to viewports with room for it.

There are no photographs anywhere on this site, and spec 0004 rejected photography outright. The
covers that ship with this work are drawn in the system's own hairline geometry, the register the
ops chart and the field figures already use.

### The body is the deviation doing its job

A dispatch body is the first thing on this site that is unambiguously long-form prose, which is what
the one recorded deviation exists for. The body sets in the proportional face. Everything around it
does not: the headline, the date, the standfirst, the topics, and — inside the body — every heading,
both list markers, all code, and any caption.

That boundary is the point. The standfirst reads like prose and is chrome, because two sentences do
not accumulate the way thirty paragraphs do, and a typeface change two lines under a title buys
nothing.

### Navigation, now that there is more than one page

The header carries four links — Projects, Experience, News, Contact — and the brand goes to the
front page rather than to the top of the current one. Below the narrow breakpoint two survive: News,
because it is the only one that is a page rather than a fragment, and Contact, because it was there
before. Still no hamburger; a drawer for four links is machinery in place of a decision.

On a News page the News link is marked as current, in ink. **Not in amber.** The signal is spent
four times already — the masthead mark, the masthead action, the current role, the scroll gauge —
and a fifth on a nav item would be the worst available fifth, because it would be permanently lit on
two of the three pages. That is precisely how an accent stops meaning "the one important thing".

The footer gains a second, separately named list of the site's pages above its list of the home
page's bands. On a dispatch page it is the only full site map.

## Decisions

**Dispatch metadata is typed data; only the body is MDX.** The alternative — metadata declared
inside the MDX file — was rejected for what it costs the test setup rather than for anything about
authoring. Recorded as ADR 0008.

**The header, the main region and the footer move to the layout.** They were the home page's; now
they are every route's. Recorded as ADR 0007, along with the consequence that every link to a band
becomes root-relative.

**Every band link is root-relative, from every place that renders one.** A bare fragment works on
the home page and points at nothing from a News page. One rule with no per-call-site judgement is
worth more than the router push it saves.

**News is band 07 and Contact renumbers to 08.** Contact has to stay last: the page is a single
argument that ends with a way to make contact, and moving that off the end breaks the page's
rhetoric rather than just its numbering. The tempting slot for News was 03, straight after Practice
— and that is exactly why it is wrong. The platforms are the substance and everything else is their
context; a handful of dispatches are not the substance, and the page's own structure should not claim
otherwise.

**The index is a page head, not a second masthead and not a numbered band.** A band would make the
index figure a decoration, and the figures are one sequence down one page's edge — two pages both
starting at 01 destroys what the figure means. There is exactly one masthead: it carries the name,
and it is the front door.

**The index is at the page's default width; a dispatch is at the reading width.** A list is chrome
and belongs at the same measure as the chrome above it. An article is a narrow measure inside a
wider frame, which is the distinction.

**Neither new page animates.** The index heading is its largest above-the-fold element and must not
fade in, for the same reason the masthead never has. Fading the list in as one block is the
scroll-animation register this system rejects, and an index is scanned rather than descended. A
dispatch that faded its paragraphs in as you scrolled would be the same mistake at length.

**The scroll gauge is left exactly as it is.** It reads the document, which on a dispatch page is
very nearly the same thing as the article — and reading progress through one long text is the
canonical use of a progress line. It is slightly short at the foot of a piece, because the document
includes the footer. Scoping it to the article instead would be more code than the problem deserves
and would make the same 2px line mean two different things on two pages.

**Topics are labels and never links.** There are no topic archives, and a badge that reads as
clickable and is not is the defect the engagement card was built to avoid.

**No previous/next navigation between dispatches, and no feed.** Both are worth having and neither
is worth guessing at now.

**No empty state.** Nothing in this content layer has one. The home band shows three, so there are
at least three, and that is asserted rather than handled.

## User stories

- As a reader who has landed on the home page, I can see that there is writing, read the three most
  recent headlines, and reach the rest in one click.
- As a reader of one piece, I can get back to the list from the top or the bottom without scrolling
  to find the way.
- As someone scanning the index, the dates line up in a column and I can read down them.
- As a keyboard user, one Tab reaches each row, the focus ring surrounds the whole row rather than
  just its headline, and Enter follows it.
- As a screen reader user, each row is one link named by its headline — not by its date, its summary
  and its topics read out in sequence — and the list announces as ordered.
- As a reader on a phone, the header still offers the two destinations that are pages, and no row is
  three-quarters cover image.
- As a reader in either theme, every pairing on both new pages meets its contrast threshold,
  including code on a sunken surface inside prose.
- As a visitor who follows a stale link, I get a page that says so, inside the site's own chrome.
- As a visitor who has asked for reduced motion, nothing on either page ever moved anyway.
- As a search engine, each of the three page kinds declares a canonical pointing at itself and the
  right Open Graph type — a profile, a listing, an article.
- As the author, publishing a piece is one typed record and one file beside it, and getting either
  half wrong fails the test run rather than the page.

## Out of scope

- An RSS or JSON feed.
- Topic archives, filtering, search, and pagination.
- Previous/next navigation between dispatches.
- Comments, reactions, view counts, and anything else needing a backend.
- Per-dispatch Open Graph images.
- Drafts, scheduling, and any notion of an unpublished piece.
- In-body images beyond the cover. The element map handles one, but nothing authored needs it yet.
- Heading anchors and a table of contents. Worth adding the day a piece is long enough to want them.

## Test plan

Automated, on every run:

- The index renders as a story in **both** themes and is checked by an accessibility engine at error
  level, as does a dispatch page and the row on its own. Neither route is story-tested directly, so
  without these their contrast would be unverified.
- Every element a body can produce — headings, both list kinds, blockquote, fenced and inline code,
  captions, rules, internal and external links — renders as one specimen story in both themes. This
  is where the chrome-versus-prose boundary is actually enforced, and where code on a sunken surface
  inside prose gets checked.
- The index page is rendered whole and asserted to contain **no hidden element at all** — a stronger
  guarantee than the home page's, because this page has no entrance animation to allow one.
- One link per row, and its accessible name is exactly the headline. This is the assertion that pins
  the whole-row target, because the obvious simplification — wrapping the row in the link — breaks
  this and nothing else.
- Rows with and without a cover keep the same number of columns, so a mixed list stays aligned.
- The metadata and the bodies are checked against each other: every record has a file, every file
  has a record, and no body smuggles in a second source of metadata, a second top-level heading, or
  an image with no dimensions.
- Dates are checked the way every other date on the site is: stored as a sortable value plus a
  written-out label, ordered newest-first, and never derived from the clock.
- The metadata module is asserted not to import a body, because that one line would put an MDX
  pipeline into a test project that has none.
- Every link the shell renders to a band is asserted to be root-relative. This is the regression
  guard for the navigation change, and the failure it catches is invisible from the home page.
- The header marks the current page once, and only on a page.

Manual, before it ships:

- Light theme first. The anchor is dark-native, so light is where it fails.
- The header at exactly the breakpoint where it goes from two links to four: no wrap, no crowding
  against the gutter.
- A band link clicked from a News page lands on the right band of the home page; the same link
  clicked from the home page still scrolls smoothly and lands below the sticky header rather than
  underneath it.
- Tab to a row: the ring surrounds the row. Click the thumbnail: it navigates.
- A body's emphasis is a real italic and not a sheared roman, and its code blocks are still
  monospace while its paragraphs are not.
- A stale URL under `/news` renders the not-found page inside the real header and footer.
- Each of the three page kinds, viewed as source: exactly one canonical, pointing at itself.
